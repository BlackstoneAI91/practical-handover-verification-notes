# Passing a Carpet Cleaning Scope Note to an Interim Coordinator

A practical guide for Sarawak halls and venues on handing a carpet cleaning scope note to an interim coordinator: who holds the current version, which approvals stay with the original role, and what must come back before the walkthrough.

Published by Blackstone Intelligence.

## Why a scope note needs a receipt, not just a forward

A scope note for carpet cleaning is easy to write and easy to lose. It usually starts as a paragraph in an email, grows into a list of rooms and edges, and then sits in one person's inbox. When the coordinator changes mid-project, the note may be forwarded without its attachments, quoted without its version date, or read as if it were the final word on every decision.

The fix is not a longer document. It is a short receipt: a record that says which version of the scope note the interim coordinator received, on what date, from whom, and what they are expected to send back before the planned walkthrough. The receipt does not transfer authority. It transfers information, and it makes the gap between the two visible.

This guide uses a fictional Sarawak hall, which we will call the Community Hall throughout, to show a one-page handover record and a copyable acknowledgement. The hall, its rooms and its people are invented for illustration. Nothing here is treatment advice, and no timing or score below is a standard; they are author heuristics you can adjust.

## Who holds the current version

Before anything is passed on, decide which copy is current. A scope note can exist in three places at once: the original coordinator's file, a shared drive, and a printed copy in the hall office. If the interim coordinator receives the printed copy while the shared drive holds a later revision, the handover has already failed.

A simple rule helps: the current version is the one with the latest revision date and a named owner. The owner is the role, not the person, so the note survives a change of staff. In the Community Hall example, the owner is the venue coordinator role, and the interim coordinator receives a copy marked as a read-only reference until the walkthrough is complete.

The receipt should name the version, the date it was issued, and the person who issued it. It should also say plainly that the interim coordinator is receiving the note for information and coordination, not as a transfer of approval. That single sentence prevents most of the confusion that follows a mid-project change.

## Which approvals stay with the original role

Some decisions belong to the role that commissioned the work, even while another person coordinates day to day. In the Community Hall example, three approvals stay with the original coordinator: any change to the rooms included in the scope, any change to the planned walkthrough date, and any instruction that adds work beyond the note.

The interim coordinator can still do useful things without those approvals. They can confirm access times, collect room keys, note which areas are in use on the day, and record questions that need an answer before the walkthrough. Separating coordination from approval keeps the handover honest: the interim coordinator is not asked to sign off on something they did not commission.

## What information is due back before the walkthrough

A handover is only complete when something comes back. Set a short list of items the interim coordinator must return before the walkthrough, and keep the list small enough to finish. In the Community Hall example, four items are due back:

First, a confirmed access list: which doors will be open, who holds the keys, and who will be on site. Second, a room-by-room note of any area that cannot be reached on the day, with the reason. Third, a list of open questions for the original coordinator, each with the name of the person who can answer it. Fourth, a signed acknowledgement that the interim coordinator has read the current version and understands that approvals remain with the original role.

These items are examples, not a required standard. A smaller venue might need only the access list and the acknowledgement. The point is that the receipt has a return path, so the handover does not end with a forwarded email.

## Worked handover record: the Community Hall

The following is an invented worked record for the fictional Community Hall. It shows how a one-page receipt might look. Names, dates and room labels are illustrative only.

Handover record
Venue: Community Hall (fictional)
Scope note: Carpet cleaning, main hall and side room
Version received: Revision 3, dated 14 March
Issued by: Venue coordinator role (original coordinator)
Received by: Interim coordinator, starting 17 March
Status of copy: Read-only reference until walkthrough
Approvals retained by original role: changes to rooms in scope; changes to walkthrough date; any added work
Due back before walkthrough: access list; unreachable-area note; open questions list; signed acknowledgement
Walkthrough planned: 24 March (example date only)

Notes column: the interim coordinator writes one line per item returned, with the date. The original coordinator initials each line when satisfied. No line is closed by a verbal comment alone.

This record is deliberately short. It fits on one page, it names the version, and it makes the retained approvals explicit. A longer record tends to be read once and then ignored.

## A copyable acknowledgement

The acknowledgement below can be copied into an email or printed at the foot of the handover record. It is a template, not legal wording, and it should be adapted to the venue's own documents.

Acknowledgement
I confirm that I have received version [number] of the carpet cleaning scope note for [venue], dated [date]. I understand that this copy is for coordination and reference. I understand that approval for changes to the rooms in scope, the walkthrough date and any added work remains with [original role]. I will return the access list, the unreachable-area note, the open questions list and this acknowledgement before the walkthrough on [date].
Name: [interim coordinator]
Role: [role]
Date: [date]

Keep the acknowledgement with the handover record, not in a separate folder. When the walkthrough happens, the two documents together show what was received, what was returned and what remained with the original role.

## Where the provider fits into the handover

A change of coordinator often happens close to the date a provider is due on site. That is when a clear scope note earns its keep. The interim coordinator should know which rooms are included, which edges and thresholds matter, and who to ask when something is unclear. They should not be asked to approve a different scope on the day.

## A short checklist before you press send

Before the scope note goes to the interim coordinator, run a short check. These are author heuristics, not an audited standard.

Is the version number and date on the copy being sent? Does the receipt name the original role that keeps approval? Is the return list short enough to finish before the walkthrough? Is there a named person for each open question? Is the acknowledgement attached rather than promised later?

If any answer is no, fix it before sending. A scope note that arrives without a receipt is just a document in an inbox. A scope note with a receipt is a handover that can be checked, and that is the difference that matters when a coordinator changes mid-project.
