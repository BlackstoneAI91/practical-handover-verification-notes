# Reconciling Two Copies of a Wafer Recycling Enquiry Before a Review Meeting

A practical version-compare guide for Singapore procurement reviewers who find two copies of the same wafer recycling enquiry with different cover dates, including a worked comparison, questions for the record owner and a note that preserves the earlier record.

Published by Blackstone Intelligence.

## Why two copies of one enquiry need reconciling before the meeting

A review meeting works best when every attendee is looking at the same document. When a wafer recycling enquiry appears twice in the packet with different cover dates, the meeting can drift into a debate about which copy is current instead of what the team actually needs to decide. The two copies may differ only in a date stamp, or they may differ in scope, quantities, reference numbers or the wording of a question. Either way, the reviewer who notices the duplication carries a small but useful responsibility: establish which copy is later, what changed between them, and what the earlier copy still tells the record.

This guide is written for Singapore procurement teams that source, receive and document semiconductor and solar materials. It stays with documentation and sourcing practice. It does not describe processing steps, regulated handling, prices or commitments, and it does not assume that any particular supplier will respond in a particular way. The aim is narrower: give you a repeatable way to reconcile two copies so the review meeting starts from a settled packet.

## Start with the cover date, but do not stop there

The cover date is the obvious difference and the easiest to over-trust. A later cover date usually suggests a later revision, but it can also mean a document was reissued with a new date and no substantive change, or that an earlier draft was reprinted after the fact. Treat the cover date as a signal, not a verdict.

A short triage before the meeting helps. Put the two copies side by side and ask three questions in order. First, is the cover date the only difference? If so, the reconciliation is mostly about recording which date supersedes which. Second, do the reference numbers match? A changed reference can indicate a separate enquiry rather than a revision, and that changes how you file both copies. Third, does either copy carry a revision marker, a version label or a note such as superseded or reissued? If one does, that marker usually outranks the cover date as evidence of intent.

If the answer to all three is unclear, do not guess. Mark the pair as unresolved, note the specific fields that differ, and carry the question to the record owner. A reviewer who says plainly that two copies exist and the difference is not yet settled is more useful to a meeting than one who quietly picks a favourite.

## A version-compare checklist for the packet

The following checklist is an author heuristic, not an established standard. Adapt it to your own filing conventions. Work through it once per duplicated enquiry and keep the completed checklist with the packet.

Identity fields: compare the enquiry reference, the sender organisation, the named contact role (not personal details), and the site or facility named. Any difference here may mean two enquiries rather than two versions.

Scope fields: compare the material categories named, the quantities stated, the units used, and any qualifiers such as grade, form or condition. A quantity that appears in one copy and not the other is a substantive difference, not a formatting one.

Question fields: compare the specific questions asked. If the later copy drops a question, note whether it was answered elsewhere or simply removed. Dropped questions are easy to lose and hard to reconstruct later.

Date and status fields: compare the cover date, any revision marker, and any status wording such as draft, for review or final. Record which copy you are treating as current and why.

Attachment references: compare any list of attachments or enclosures by name and count. Do not open or describe confidential enclosures in the shared packet; note only that a reference differs.

Sign-off fields: compare who prepared each copy and whether either carries an approval marker. Keep this at the level of roles and markers, not personal contact details.

Score the checklist loosely. A pair that differs only in cover date is a light reconciliation. A pair that differs in scope, questions and attachments is a heavy one and probably deserves its own line in the meeting agenda.

## Worked example: one enquiry, two cover dates

The following is a fictional illustration for practice. It does not describe a real enquiry, a real supplier or a real outcome.

Suppose a reviewer receives a packet containing two copies of an enquiry about wafer recycling services. Copy A carries a cover date of 3 March and asks about two material categories, states quantities in one unit, and includes four questions. Copy B carries a cover date of 11 March, asks about the same two categories, states quantities in a different unit, and includes three questions. Both carry the same enquiry reference. Neither carries a revision marker.

A quick comparison produces four findings. The reference matches, so this is probably one enquiry rather than two. The cover date suggests Copy B is later. The unit change is a substantive difference that must be resolved before any figure is discussed. One question present in Copy A is absent from Copy B, and nothing in Copy B says it was answered.

The reviewer does not decide which unit is correct. Instead, the reviewer records the four findings, marks Copy B as the probable current version pending confirmation, and flags the missing question and the unit change as open items. At the meeting, the packet shows one current copy, one preserved earlier copy, and a short list of differences. The discussion can then focus on the open items rather than on which document to trust.

This example shows why the cover date alone is insufficient. The most consequential differences here are the unit change and the dropped question, and neither is visible from the date stamp.

## The question to put to the record owner

Every duplicated enquiry has a record owner: the person or role that prepared the original and can say what changed. Before the meeting, send that owner a short, specific question rather than a general request to confirm. A useful pattern is to state the two copies, the fields that differ, and the single decision you need.

For example: two copies of the enquiry are in the packet, dated 3 March and 11 March. The quantities are stated in different units and one question appears only in the earlier copy. Please confirm which copy is current, whether the unit change was intentional, and whether the missing question was answered or withdrawn.

Three habits make this question easier to answer. Name the fields rather than describing the documents in general terms. Ask for a decision, not an opinion. And keep the question to roles and references rather than personal contact details, so the exchange can be filed with the packet.

If the owner cannot respond before the meeting, say so in the packet. An unresolved question that is visibly unresolved is safer than a silent assumption. The meeting can then decide whether to proceed on the probable current copy or to defer the item.

## Writing the note that preserves the earlier record

Reconciling two copies does not mean discarding one. The earlier copy often holds the original wording of a question, the first statement of a quantity, or the context that explains why the enquiry was raised at all. Preserve it, and write a short note that explains the relationship between the two.

A workable note has four parts. State that two copies exist and give their cover dates. State which copy is treated as current and on what basis, such as a later date or a revision marker. List the differences found in the checklist. State what remains open and who is expected to resolve it.

Keep the note factual and short. Avoid words that imply a decision has been made when it has not. Avoid restating the full contents of either copy; the note is a pointer, not a replacement. File the note with both copies so that a colleague opening the folder later can see the relationship without reconstructing it.

This practice also helps when the enquiry concerns materials that move between industries. San Techno describes materials rejected from one tightly controlled process becoming valuable inputs for another, and frames its silicon recovery work around integrated-circuit fabs, silicon wafer manufacturers, solar cell companies and chip packaging facilities. That framing is useful when you need to explain, in the note, why an enquiry spans more than one material category. It does not change the documentation rule: the note records what your packet contains, not what a supplier might do.

## Keeping the reconciled packet clean for the meeting

Once the two copies are reconciled, the packet should read as one story. Put the current copy first, the preserved earlier copy second, and the reconciliation note on top. Remove duplicate attachments only if the note records that they were duplicates; otherwise leave them in place.

Add a single line to the agenda naming the reconciliation and the open items. This gives the meeting a place to confirm or correct the reviewer's reading without reopening the whole packet. If the record owner has responded, attach the response to the note rather than editing the note to match it, so the sequence of events stays visible.

After the meeting, update the note with the outcome: which copy was confirmed current, how the unit question was settled, and whether the missing question was answered or withdrawn. This closes the loop and gives the next reviewer a clean starting point.

A final point on scope. This guide covers documentation and sourcing practice only. It does not describe processing steps, regulated handling, prices or commitments, and it does not predict how any supplier will respond. Where an enquiry touches wafer recycling services, the reviewer's job is to make sure the question is clearly stated and the record is clean, not to answer it on the supplier's behalf.
