# Retiring an Old Online Dog Food Comparison When the Source Listing Splits

A practical note for Malaysian households whose saved online dog food comparison has split into two source pages: what survives, what to re-check on each new page, and how to archive the old row with a clear reason.

Published by Blackstone Intelligence.

## Why a Saved Comparison Needs a Retirement Note

A saved comparison of online pet food is a working document, and working documents drift. Someone saves a listing, another person adds a note, a third person screenshots a variant, and then one day the saved page no longer matches what is on screen. One common form of drift is a split: a single source listing becomes two distinct pages, perhaps because a format, pack size or variant has been separated out, or because a seller has reorganised how it presents a range.

When that happens, the temptation is to keep the old row and quietly add the new pages beside it. That produces a comparison with two or three rows that look like separate options but may describe overlapping things. A cleaner habit is to retire the old row deliberately, with a short written reason, and rebuild the comparison from the new pages.

## What a Split Actually Changes

A split changes the unit of comparison. Before the split, one saved row stood for one listing. After the split, two pages may each carry part of what the old row described. The old row is now ambiguous: it is not wrong, exactly, but it no longer maps to a single page you can open and check.

It helps to separate two kinds of detail in any saved row. There are details that belong to the source page, such as the page address, the listing title as displayed, the variant selector options, the pack size shown and the delivery terms stated on that page. And there are details that belong to your household, such as why you saved it, who in the family was going to check it, and what you still needed to confirm.

When the listing splits, the source details must be re-collected from each new page. The household details survive, because they were never on the listing. That distinction is the whole basis of a clean retirement: keep your reasons, re-collect the source facts.

## The Detail That Survives the Split

In most splits, one thing carries over reliably: the reason the row was saved. If the note said the household wanted to compare delivery terms for a particular pack size, that intent survives even if the pack size now appears on a different page. If the note said someone wanted to check the variant selector before deciding, that check still applies to whichever new page now carries the variant.

What does not survive is any assumption that the old page's displayed details still describe the new pages. A split is precisely the moment to stop trusting the old screenshot. Treat the old row as a pointer to your intent, not as a record of current source facts.

A useful habit is to write the surviving intent as a single sentence at the top of the new comparison, before adding any rows. For example: we are comparing delivery terms and pack size for a household of two adults and one medium dog, and we will check the variant selector on each page before shortlisting. That sentence is yours, it is stable, and it gives every new row something to be measured against.

## What to Check on Each New Page

Once the listing has split, each new page needs its own check. The checks below are record-keeping steps, not buying advice, and they are deliberately about what a page states rather than what a product is like.

First, confirm the page identity. Note the page address and the listing title exactly as displayed, because two pages from the same source can carry very similar titles. Second, read the variant selector. If the old row referred to a specific variant, find where that variant now sits, and note whether it appears on one new page, both, or neither. Third, record the pack size as stated on that page, in the units the page uses.

Fourth, note the delivery terms as displayed on that page, including any stated delivery area or timing language, without assuming they apply to the other new page. Fifth, note the date and time you checked, because a split page can be edited again. Sixth, note anything the page leaves unclear, and leave it as a gap rather than filling it in from memory.

## Recording Why a Row Is Archived

An archived row should explain itself to whoever opens the comparison next, including you in a month. A short archive note with four parts is usually enough.

State what the row was. For example: saved row for a medium-pack dry dog food listing, checked on a stated date. State what changed. For example: the source listing now resolves to two pages, one carrying the smaller pack and one carrying the larger pack. State what was carried forward. For example: the household intent sentence and the delivery-terms question moved to the new comparison. State what was not carried forward. For example: the old page's displayed variant list and pack size, because they were not re-verified on the new pages.

Keep the archived row visible rather than deleting it. A deleted row loses the history, and the next person may re-add the same listing without knowing it was retired. A visible archive with a reason is a small piece of institutional memory for a household.

If the split was caused by a seller reorganising a range, the archive note should say so only if the page itself makes that clear. Otherwise, describe what you observed, not why it happened.

## A Worked Example With Fictional Labels

The following example uses invented labels and invented page names. It does not describe any real merchant, product, price or stock level.

Suppose a household saved a comparison row labelled simply as the medium dry food listing from a source they called Page A. The note said: compare delivery terms, check the variant selector, ask whether the larger pack is worth considering. Weeks later, Page A no longer matches the saved screenshot. The source now presents two pages, which the household labels Page A1 and Page A2.

On Page A1, the household records the page title as displayed, finds the smaller pack size in the variant selector, and notes the delivery terms stated there. On Page A2, they record a different title, find the larger pack size, and note the delivery terms stated there, which are worded differently from Page A1. Neither page is assumed to inherit the other's terms.

The old row is then archived with a note: retired because the source listing split into two pages; household intent sentence carried forward; old variant list and pack size not carried forward because they were not re-verified; new rows created for Page A1 and Page A2 with check dates. The comparison now has two rows that each point to a page someone can open, plus one archived row that explains the history.

Notice what the example does not do. It does not decide which page is better, does not compare prices, and does not claim either page is in stock. It only records what each page states and why the old row was retired.

## A Short Checklist for the Next Split

Keep this sequence nearby for the next time a saved listing divides. It is an author heuristic, not a tested procedure, and the timings are suggestions only.

Within a day of noticing the split, open both new pages and record page identity, variant options, pack size, delivery terms and the check date. Within the same sitting, write the household intent sentence at the top of the new comparison. Then archive the old row with the four-part note: what it was, what changed, what carried forward, what did not.

Before shortlisting anything, re-read the archived note and confirm that every new row has a check date and a page identity. If a new page leaves something unclear, leave the gap visible rather than filling it from the old screenshot. Finally, if the comparison is shared with other people, tell them the old row is retired and why, so nobody re-adds it by accident.

A comparison that records its own history is easier to trust than one that silently changes. Retiring a row is not losing information; it is keeping the information honest.
